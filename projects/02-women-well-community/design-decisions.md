# Design Decisions — Women Well Community (WWC)

**Project:** Community Health Infographic Design  
**Approach:** AI-Assisted Visual Communication  
**Design Variants:** Illustrated Community Infographic and Professional Proposal Infographic

## 1. Communication Challenge

Women Well Community (WWC) is a proposed six-week preventive-health initiative for women aged 25–55 in Singapore.

The communication challenge was to present a multi-component programme in a visually accessible format while retaining its purpose, intended audience and proposed activities.

The programme concept includes three connected elements:

**Awareness → Practical Action → Peer Support**

Two visual approaches were retained to explore how presentation style can influence the clarity, tone and perceived accessibility of the same information.

## 2. Design A — Illustrated Community Infographic

**Visual characteristics:**
- Pastel lavender, pink and green
- Friendly illustrations and decorative motifs
- Rounded information panels
- Warm, approachable presentation

**Design rationale:**

The illustrated approach uses softer colours and informal visual elements to make the proposed programme feel welcoming and relatable.

This style may be suitable for introducing the programme to potential community participants, particularly when the objective is to encourage initial interest.

**Potential limitation:**

Decorative elements and dense text may compete for attention. Readability should be checked at realistic viewing sizes.

## 3. Design B — Professional Proposal Infographic

**Visual characteristics:**
- Teal and white colour palette
- Structured information panels
- Lifestyle photography
- Modern typography and clearer section separation

**Design rationale:**

The professional approach places greater emphasis on structure and information hierarchy.

It may be more suitable for explaining the programme concept to stakeholders who need to understand its purpose, target audience and proposed delivery model.

**Potential limitation:**

A more formal presentation may feel less personal to some community audiences. Small text and information density also require review.

## 4. Comparative Design Evaluation

| Dimension | Illustrated Version | Professional Version |
|---|---|---|
| Communication Tone | Friendly and inviting | Formal and informative |
| Visual Identity | Pastel illustrations | Teal editorial styling |
| Main Strength | Emotional accessibility | Structured presentation |
| Potential Use | Participant-facing communication | Proposal or stakeholder communication |
| Main Trade-off | Decoration versus readability | Formality versus warmth |

These are qualitative design assessments, not findings from user testing or measured audience responses.

## 5. AI-Assisted Workflow and Human Judgement

ChatGPT supported the development of the visual materials through AI-assisted content and image generation.

Human direction remained important in defining the programme concept, identifying the intended audience, selecting visual approaches, reviewing the generated materials and deciding which versions to include in the portfolio.

The public portfolio editions were also generalised to avoid suggesting institutional affiliation, approval or implementation.

## 6. Key Learning

This project illustrates that visual communication is not simply about making information attractive.

A useful design must consider:

- Who needs the information
- What they need to understand
- Which information deserves emphasis
- How visual tone affects the presentation
- What limitations remain in AI-generated artwork

Retaining both designs allows a comparison of different communication approaches without claiming that either has been proven more effective.
